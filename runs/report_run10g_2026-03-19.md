### 24. Desync Fix Activations

**Expected for v1 CG: 0 activations total.**
**Observed: 187 activations across 20 Cell B episodes.**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|------:|
| Total activations | 187 |
| Episodes with ≥1 activation | 15/20 (75%) |
| Maximum in single episode | 93 (ep14, seed 1435) |
| Second highest | 58 (ep16, seed 1437) |
| Median per episode | 1.5 |
| Mean per episode | 9.4 |
| Mean excl. outliers (ep14, ep16) | 2.0 |

**Outlier episodes:**
- ep14 (seed 1435): 93 desyncs, 95 steps, WRONG (timeout). Nearly 1 desync per step. This episode was likely trapped in a degenerate CG loop where the agent repeatedly requested invalid transitions.
- ep16 (seed 1437): 58 desyncs, 61 steps, **CORRECT**. Despite massive desync activity, the agent still solved the task — suggesting the correction messages did not prevent success.

**Investigation:** The desync fix fires when the CG engine rejects a transition request. For v1 CG (simple 4-state linear graph IE→CT→HE→V), valid transitions are: IE→CT, CT→HE, HE→V, and backwards under specific conditions. Any non-adjacent transition request triggers the fix. The high activation count suggests that agents running under v1 CG are frequently requesting invalid transitions — possibly because the agent's reasoning is outpacing the CG's linear structure (e.g., wanting to jump from IE directly to HE when the agent has already identified relevant objects).

**Impact assessment:** The desync fix injects a correction message that redirects the agent. This is behaviorally different from the 10f environment, where invalid transition requests were silently ignored. The fix was introduced between 10f and 10g and was designed to be benign on v1 CG (where it was expected to fire 0 times). The high activation count means:

1. **10g Cell B episodes experienced different prompting than 10f Cell B episodes** — the correction messages were injected into conversations.
2. **This could explain Cell B's regression** from 40% (pilot) to 25% (fresh) — the correction messages may be disruptive.
3. **However,** the desync count does not correlate with success: ep16 had 58 desyncs and succeeded; ep14 had 93 and failed. Ep0 had 2 desyncs and succeeded; ep4 had 0 and failed.

**Recommendation:** This is a confound that weakens the pooled analysis. The 10f pilot data was collected without desync corrections; the 10g fresh data was collected with them active. Pooling these is methodologically questionable if the correction messages affect agent behavior. A follow-up run with the desync fix disabled (or gated behind a v1 CG exclusion) would clarify whether the fix is responsible for the regression.

## Synthesis

### 26. Decision

Per the decision framework (planning prompt Section 4.8):

**→ "full_cg_db ≥ 30% AND separation 5–15pp"** (pooled: 30% vs 20%, +10pp)

> Effect is real but smaller than pilot suggested. Report as a trend. Likely not worth powering further.

However, the fresh-data-only result (+5pp) and the batch-direction reversal (Batch 1: −10pp, Batch 2: +20pp) suggest the pooled +10pp is substantially driven by the pilot data. If only fresh data is considered, the result maps to:

**→ "Both agents 20–30%, separation < 5pp"** (fresh: 25% vs 20%, +5pp)

> Pilot effect was noise. The architecture does not help at 100 steps once sampling variability washes out.

**The most honest assessment is between these two categories.** There is a directional trend (+10pp pooled, +5pp fresh) favoring the architecture, but it is not statistically significant, not robust across batches, and potentially confounded by the desync fix.

### 27. Comparison to 10f Pilot

| Metric | 10f Pilot | 10g Fresh | Change |
|--------|:---------:|:---------:|:------:|
| base_llm accuracy | 20% | 20% | 0pp |
| full_cg_db accuracy | 40% | 25% | −15pp |
| Architecture advantage | +20pp | +5pp | −15pp |

**The base_llm rate is perfectly consistent** (20% in both datasets), confirming environmental stability for the unscaffolded agent.

**The full_cg_db rate declined** from 40% to 25%. Two factors may contribute:
1. **Pilot inflation:** The 10f pilot's 40% was at the high end of the binomial CI [12%, 74%]. The true rate was likely lower.
2. **Desync fix confound:** The 10g fresh data was collected with 187 desync correction messages injected into Cell B conversations. These did not exist during the 10f pilot.

### 28. Premature Convergence Investigation

The 10f report hypothesized that DB-driven premature convergence hurt full_cg_db at 125 steps. At 100 steps:

- Cell B's wrong-order failure rate (4/15 = 27%) is higher than Cell A's (2/16 = 13%), consistent with agents that identify correct objects but commit to wrong orderings.
- Cell B's step-budget comparison (seeds 1421–1430) shows zero within-seed concordance between 100 and 125 steps, suggesting that failure is stochastic rather than systematically linked to premature convergence.

The premature convergence hypothesis is not strongly supported at 100 steps. The dominant failure mode for both agents is timeout (60%), indicating the primary bottleneck is insufficient exploration time, not premature commitment.

### 29. Paper Implications

The 10g result weakens the case for including 4-object ORDER_SENSITIVE as a standalone finding. The pilot's +20pp was suggestive, but the powered replication shows:

- A non-significant +10pp pooled (p=0.276)
- Only +5pp in fresh data
- A potential desync fix confound
- Contradictory batch effects

**For the benchmark paper, this result is most honestly reported as:** "On 4-object deterministic ordering, we observed a non-significant trend favoring the scaffolded agent (pooled: 30% vs 20%, Fisher p=0.276). The pilot estimate of +20pp shrank to +5pp in fresh data, consistent with pilot inflation."

This can still contribute to the deterministic-vs-stochastic narrative alongside the 11-series, but as a suggestive contrast rather than a confirmed finding.

### 30. Recommended Next Steps

**A. Desync fix isolation (highest priority).** The 187 desync activations on v1 CG are unexpected and represent a confound. Two options:
1. Gate the desync fix behind `cg_version != "v1"` so it only fires on v2+ CG
2. Re-run 10 episodes of Cell B (seeds 1421–1430) with the fix disabled and compare to the 10g result

**B. Close out 4-object ORDER_SENSITIVE.** Unless the desync fix isolation reveals a large effect, the architectural advantage at 4-object deterministic ordering is too small and noisy to anchor a paper finding. Report as a trend in the cross-experiment comparison table.

**C. Pivot to compositional rules.** The extended benchmark series (10d–10g, 11a–11e) has thoroughly explored the ordering dimension. The next frontier is compositional rules (e.g., "A AND (B before C)"), where the architecture's structured exploration may provide a clearer advantage.
