# Run 10b Report: Gradual Periodic Feedback Order-Sensitive Pilot

**Date:** 2026-03-11
**Design:** 4 cells x 15 episodes = 60 total
**Seed:** 1051 | **Total cost:** $73.92
**Planning prompt:** [prompt_run10b.md](prompt_run10b.md)

---

## 1. Overview

Run 10b tests whether **gradual object revelation** — confirming one correct object per 5-step checkpoint — combined with both 3-object and 4-object ordering conditions produces architectural separation. This addresses Run 10's ceiling problem: full reveal of all 3 correct objects at step 5 collapsed the task into pure permutation search (6 orderings), and both agents converged at 86.7% with 40 steps.

The gradual feedback mechanism reviews the agent's cumulative exploration history at regular intervals and confirms one previously-tested correct object per checkpoint. This keeps agents in a joint inference problem — simultaneously narrowing *which objects* and *in what arrangement* — for most of the episode. Objects are confirmed in first-tested order to ensure determinism.

### 1.1 Design

| Cell | Agent | N Objects | Required Order | Max Steps | Episodes |
|------|-------|:---------:|:--------------:|:---------:|:--------:|
| A | base_llm | 3 (A→D→C) | [A, D, C] | 60 | 15 |
| B | full_cg_db | 3 (A→D→C) | [A, D, C] | 60 | 15 |
| C | base_llm | 4 (A→B→E→C) | [A, B, E, C] | 60 | 15 |
| D | full_cg_db | 4 (A→B→E→C) | [A, B, E, C] | 60 | 15 |

### 1.2 Configuration

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Objects | 5 (A, B, C, D, E) |
| Rule type | ORDER_SENSITIVE |
| Gradual feedback interval | Every 5 steps |
| Gradual feedback mode | 1 correct object per checkpoint, first-tested priority |
| Feedback suppression | After any detector activation |
| Competitive inhibition | Enabled (winner-take-most) |
| Action model | Claude Sonnet 4.5 |
| Trigger model | Claude Haiku 4.5 |

## 3. Gradual Feedback Timeline

Gradual feedback was **perfectly deterministic** across all 60 episodes. Because agents consistently tested all 5 objects in early exploration, every checkpoint had eligible objects.

### 3.1 Revelation Schedule

**3-object episodes (Cells A, B):**

| Step | Object Confirmed | Confirmed Set After |
|:----:|:----------------:|:-------------------:|
| 5 | A | {A} |
| 10 | C | {A, C} |
| 15 | D | {A, C, D} — complete |

**4-object episodes (Cells C, D):**

| Step | Object Confirmed | Confirmed Set After |
|:----:|:----------------:|:-------------------:|
| 5 | A | {A} |
| 10 | B | {A, B} |
| 15 | C | {A, B, C} |
| 20 | E | {A, B, C, E} — complete |

### 3.2 Effective Search Budget

After all correct objects are confirmed, the remaining steps are the "effective search budget" for permutation search.

| Condition | Objects Confirmed By | Max Steps | Effective Search Budget | Permutations |
|-----------|:--------------------:|:---------:|:----------------------:|:------------:|
| 3-obj | Step 15 | 60 | **45 steps** | 6 |
| 4-obj | Step 20 | 60 | **40 steps** | 24 |

At 3-obj, 45 steps for 6 permutations is generous — consistent with Cell A achieving 53.3%. At 4-obj, 40 steps for 24 permutations (~1.7 steps per permutation) is tight. Testing each permutation requires at least 4 place actions + 4 remove actions (8 steps per cycle), so agents can test at most ~5 permutations — a 20.8% chance of finding the answer by random sampling.

### 3.3 No Activation-Based Suppression

No detector activations occurred before feedback delivery in any episode. This is expected — order-sensitive conditions require all objects in the correct sequence, and agents testing subsets or wrong orderings never trigger the detector during the exploration phase.

CG+DB reduced the timeout rate by **46.7 percentage points** (80% → 33%) but increased the wrong-order rate by **40 percentage points** (20% → 60%). The architecture provides enough structure to formulate and submit an answer, but the answers are overwhelmingly wrong on order.

## 5. Architectural Analysis (Cells B & D)

### 5.1 Dynamic Behavior Fire Rates

Across 30 CG+DB episodes, 64 DB fires occurred (avg 2.13/episode).

| Trigger | Count | % | Typical Step Range |
|---------|:-----:|:--:|:------------------:|
| `exploration_stagnation` | 30 | 46.9% | Steps 6-11 (early) |
| `order_hypothesis` | 29 | 45.3% | Steps 12-19 (mid) |
| `rule_change_hypothesis` | 5 | 7.8% | Steps 44-58 (late) |

The `order_hypothesis` DB fired in nearly every CG+DB episode (29/30), typically at steps 12-19 — aligning closely with gradual feedback completion at steps 15 (3-obj) and 20 (4-obj). This suggests the DB correctly detects order-relevant evidence as confirmed objects are tested in different arrangements.

The `rule_change_hypothesis` DB fired 5 times late in episodes (steps 44-58), all false positives — there is no rule change.

### 5.2 Context Graph State Transitions

94 CG transitions across 30 episodes (avg 3.13/episode). 20/30 episodes (66.7%) reached ORDER_TESTING.

**Most common progression:**
```
INITIAL_EXPLORATION (step 0)
  → COMBINATION_TESTING (step ~9)
  → DIMENSION_DISCOVERY (step ~11)
  → ORDER_TESTING (step ~14)
  → HYPOTHESIS_EVALUATION (step ~47)
  → VERIFICATION (step ~55)
```

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| ORDER_TESTING entry (median step) | 14 |
| Episodes reaching ORDER_TESTING | 20/30 (67%) |
| Episodes reaching VERIFICATION | 20/30 (67%) |

### 5.3 The One 4-Object Success (Cell D, Episode 0)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Steps taken | 49 (11 below limit) |
| Guessed order | [A, B, E, C] — correct |
| CG transitions | 1 (stayed in INITIAL_EXPLORATION) |
| ORDER_TESTING reached | **No** |

This success is anomalous: the agent solved the 4-object order problem while staying in INITIAL_EXPLORATION — never entering ORDER_TESTING. It used only 49 steps while the average Cell D episode used 59.2. This appears to be "lucky efficiency" rather than systematic exploration, and does not validate the ORDER_TESTING mechanism.

By contrast, Cell D episode 1 (a failure) reached ORDER_TESTING at step 17 and spent 30 steps there (steps 17-47), yet still guessed the wrong order. ORDER_TESTING entry is necessary but not sufficient.

## 6. Cross-Run Comparison

### 6.1 Accuracy Matrix

|                | Run 09 (no FB) | Run 10 (full reveal) | Run 10b (gradual) |
|----------------|:--------------:|:--------------------:|:-----------------:|
| **3-obj base_llm** | 6.7% | 86.7% (40 steps) | **53.3%** (60 steps) |
| **3-obj full_cg_db** | 0.0% | 86.7% (40 steps) | **26.7%** (60 steps) |
| **4-obj base_llm** | 0.0% | — | **0.0%** (60 steps) |
| **4-obj full_cg_db** | 0.0% | — | **6.7%** (60 steps) |

### 6.2 Interpretation

Three clear conclusions emerge:

1. **Gradual revelation is harder than full revelation.** Run 10's full reveal at 40 steps produced 86.7% for both agents at 3-obj. Run 10b's gradual reveal at 60 steps produced only 53.3% (base_llm) and 26.7% (full_cg_db). Even with 20 additional steps, gradual revelation is substantially harder — the mechanism successfully creates a more demanding joint inference problem.

2. **Separation emerged but in the wrong direction at 3-obj.** Base_llm outperforms full_cg_db by 26.6pp. The CG+DB architecture's structured phases and DB fires appear to impose overhead that hurts at 3-obj complexity. The agent spends steps on CG state transitions and DB-triggered hypothesis revisions rather than directly testing orderings.

3. **At 4-obj, a qualitative architectural benefit appears in object identification.** Full_cg_db identified correct objects in 67% of episodes versus 20% for base_llm, and submitted answers in 60% versus 20%. But this object-identification advantage does not translate to order accuracy (7% vs 0%) because 24 permutations remain intractable for both agents.

## 9. Conclusions and Next Steps

### 9.1 What We Learned

1. **Gradual feedback works as designed.** It creates a genuine joint-inference challenge (no ceiling effect) and paces object revelation across multiple checkpoints.

2. **CG+DB architecture hurts at 3-obj.** The overhead of structured state transitions, DB evaluations, and hypothesis formation costs steps without providing benefit at 3-object/6-permutation complexity. The base_llm's unstructured exploration is more efficient here.

3. **CG+DB architecture helps with object identification at 4-obj.** The 67% vs 20% objects-correct gap suggests the architecture genuinely aids the "which objects?" phase of the task.

4. **Neither agent cracks 24 permutations.** The order-discovery challenge at 4-obj is qualitatively harder than at 3-obj, and neither architecture has a viable strategy for systematic permutation search within 40 effective steps.

5. **Alphabetical ordering bias is real.** 33% of Cell D's wrong-order guesses were [A, B, C, E] — the alphabetical ordering. The non-alphabetical [A, B, E, C] true rule exposes LLM priors.

### 9.2 Recommended Next Steps

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|:--------:|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Close out 3-obj order-sensitive** | Base_llm consistently outperforms CG+DB; this is not a separation condition |
| 2 | **Investigate the object-identification gap at 4-obj** | 67% vs 20% is a strong signal worth powering up (n=30-50) |
| 3 | **Consider full reveal at 4-obj** (Run 10 approach at 4 objects) | Isolate whether the permutation search itself produces separation when objects are given |
| 4 | **Fix db_notifications trace logging** | DB fires are logged to stderr but not captured in JSON traces |

### 9.3 Open Questions

- Why does the CG+DB architecture hurt at 3-obj? Is it the step cost of DB evaluations, the quality of ORDER_TESTING prompts, or the agent getting confused by CG transition notifications?
- Would 80 steps at 4-obj with gradual reveal produce separation? The 40-step effective search budget is tight for 24 permutations.
- Can the ORDER_TESTING state be improved to implement actual systematic permutation elimination rather than heuristic search?
