## Summary

The pilot produced a critical finding that reframes the Run 4 design: **base_llm never exceeds 4 successful activations**, meaning switch thresholds of 5, 7, and 9 are all unreachable. The bimodal exploration regime observed at switch=3 in Runs 3/3B was an artifact of 3 being at the upper edge of base_llm's natural exploration range (2-4 activations), not a general property. The predicted base_llm degradation at higher switch points cannot occur because the switch is never triggered.

Only full_cg_db triggers the switch, and only at decreasing rates: 40% at sw=5, 7% at sw=7. When the switch does trigger, post-switch runway determines success: switched episodes with fewer than 10 post-switch steps always fail.

## 2. Question 1: Does the Bimodal Exploration Regime Persist?

**Answer: No. The bimodal regime was an artifact of switch=3 coinciding with base_llm's natural exploration ceiling.**

### Activation Count Distributions

| Switch N | Agent | N eps | < N | = N | = N+1 | >= N+2 | "Stop at exactly N" rate |
|:--------:|-------|:-----:|:---:|:---:|:-----:|:------:|:------------------------:|
| 3 (Runs 3+3B) | base_llm | 60 | 8 (13%) | 9 (15%) | 0 (0%) | 43 (72%) | **15.0%** |
| 5 | base_llm | 15 | 15 (100%) | 0 (0%) | 0 (0%) | 0 (0%) | **0%** |
| 7 | base_llm | 15 | 15 (100%) | 0 (0%) | 0 (0%) | 0 (0%) | **0%** |
| 9 | base_llm | 15 | 15 (100%) | 0 (0%) | 0 (0%) | 0 (0%) | **0%** |
| 5 | full_cg_db | 15 | 9 (60%) | 2 (13%) | 0 (0%) | 4 (27%) | 13% |
| 7 | full_cg_db | 15 | 14 (93%) | 0 (0%) | 0 (0%) | 1 (7%) | 0% |

### base_llm Activation Count Distributions (all episodes)

| Switch N | All counts | Mean | Max |
|:--------:|-----------|:----:|:---:|
| 5 | [2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4] | 3.1 | 4 |
| 7 | [2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4] | 3.0 | 4 |
| 9 | [2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 4] | 3.1 | 4 |

**Key finding:** base_llm activation counts are **identical across all three switch thresholds** — always 2-4, mean ~3.1. The switch threshold has no effect on base_llm exploration behavior because the threshold is never reached. base_llm solves the conjunctive {A,B} rule quickly, confirms it with 2-4 test combinations, and submits.

The bimodal pattern at switch=3 in Runs 3+3B was not a deep property of the exploration dynamic — it was a coincidence that N=3 sat right at the boundary of base_llm's natural exploration range (2-4 activations). Episodes landing on exactly 3 were unlucky draws from the same distribution we see here; they triggered the switch and immediately submitted the now-wrong answer.

**Accuracy conditional on activation count:** All 45 base_llm episodes are correct regardless of activation count. The deterministic separation (< N always correct, = N always wrong) from sw=3 does not arise because no episodes reach N.

### Decision criteria outcome

The pilot matches the **"stop at exactly N" rate decreases** scenario from the planning prompt (Section 4.1): the rate drops from 15% at N=3 to 0% at N=5/7/9. This is the extreme version — not just a decrease but a complete disappearance. The base_llm never invests enough to reach higher thresholds.

**The 100% PPV claim from Runs 3+3B is broken.** Episode sw5/ep003 had RC fire at step 29 but the agent still submitted the wrong answer at step 31 — the RC fired too late (only 2 steps before submission) for the agent to revise. ### RC False Positive Analysis

Three episodes show RC firing in non-switched episodes:
- **sw5/ep014** (4 activations, RC at step 11): The agent explored enough to generate activation patterns that triggered the DB, but stopped at 4 activations before reaching the sw=5 threshold.
- **sw7/ep011** (6 activations, RC at step 31): High activation count (6) created sufficient pattern variability for the DB to trigger. The agent was approaching the sw=7 threshold.
- **sw7/ep012** (4 activations, RC at step 12): Early RC fire on limited data, likely a low-evidence trigger.

These false positives are informative: they suggest the DB has a non-trivial false alarm rate (~12%) when agents explore extensively without the rule actually changing. However, all three false-positive episodes still produced correct answers, so the false alarms did not cause failures.

## 5. Question 4: What Switch Points Should Run 4 Use?

### Step 1: Eliminate Infeasible Switch Points

All tested switch points (5, 7, 9) are technically feasible for full_cg_db:
- No step-cap episodes at any threshold
- DB fire rate >= 80% at sw=5 and sw=7 (for switched episodes)
- **However:** sw=5 and sw=7 are not true "switch points" for base_llm — they are unreachable

sw=9 was only tested with base_llm, where it is also unreachable (max activation = 4). Its feasibility for full_cg_db is unknown but can be inferred: if full_cg_db reaches the threshold only 7% of the time at sw=7, it would reach sw=9 even less frequently (likely < 5%), making it impractical.

### Step 2: Assess the Gradient

| Switch N | base_llm "stop at exactly N" rate | base_llm accuracy |
|:--------:|:---------------------------------:|:-----------------:|
| 3 (prior data) | 15% | 80-93% (varies by batch) |
| 5 (pilot) | 0% | 100% |
| 7 (pilot) | 0% | 100% |
| 9 (pilot) | 0% | 100% |

There is **no gradient at N >= 5**. The transition is binary: at N=3, some base_llm episodes hit the threshold and fail; at N >= 5, none do. The predicted monotonic degradation (stronger prior commitment -> higher "stop at exactly N" rate) does not occur because the mechanism is different from what was hypothesized:

- **Hypothesis:** Higher N means the agent is more committed to the original rule and more likely to stop at exactly N.
- **Reality:** base_llm explores the same amount regardless of N (2-4 activations). The "stop at exactly N" phenomenon at sw=3 was not caused by commitment — it was caused by the coincidence that N=3 falls within the natural exploration range.

### Step 3: Synthesis and Recommendation

The switch-point manipulation as originally conceived — varying N to create a gradient in base_llm accuracy — **does not work**. base_llm's exploration is bounded at 4 activations regardless of the switch threshold.

**The pilot reveals a different, more interesting story:**

1. **base_llm has a hard exploration ceiling of 4 activations.** It identifies the conjunctive rule in 2-3 activations and confirms it with 1-2 more. It does not explore beyond what is needed for confirmation.

2. **CG-driven agents (full_cg_db) explore far beyond the confirmation threshold.** They achieve 4-14 activations because the context graph's structured exploration drives continued testing. This additional exploration is what creates vulnerability to the hidden moderator.

3. **The hidden moderator selectively penalizes thorough exploration.** The more an agent explores (a generally desirable property), the more likely it is to trigger the rule change. This creates a paradox: the architectural features that enable deeper causal understanding (CG, DB) also expose the agent to more environmental perturbations.

4. **When the switch triggers, success depends on post-switch runway.** Episodes with >= 25 post-switch steps always succeed; episodes with < 10 always fail.

### Recommended Run 4 Design

Given that the switch-point gradient is absent, the pilot supports **Contingency Alternative A** from the planning prompt: simplify the design.

**Recommended approach — two-cell contrast at switch=3:**

| Cell | Agent | Switch N | Episodes | Purpose |
|------|-------|:--------:|:--------:|---------|
| 1 | base_llm | 3 | 60 | Baseline: some episodes encounter switch, most do not |
| 2 | cg_only | 3 | 60 | CG exploration without DB: do they trigger + fail more? |
| 3 | full_cg_db | 3 | 60 | Full architecture: triggers switch + has DB to detect it |

**Rationale:**
- sw=3 is the **only** threshold where base_llm encounters the switch (15% of episodes)
- Combined with Runs 3+3B (60 episodes per agent at sw=3), this would give n=120 per agent — adequate statistical power
- The story becomes about **differential exploration depth** (base_llm stops at 2-4; CG agents explore further) and **architectural rescue** (DB detects the rule change for full_cg_db)
- No switch-point factor needed; the agent type IS the manipulation

**Alternative — if switch-point variation is still desired:**

| Cell | Agent | Switch N | Episodes | Purpose |
|------|-------|:--------:|:--------:|---------|
| 1-3 | all 3 agents | 3 | 40 each | Replication + extension of sw=3 data |
| 4-6 | all 3 agents | 5 | 40 each | Only CG agents encounter switch; base_llm is automatic control |

This uses sw=5 as a "CG-selective" difficulty level where only CG agents face the perturbation, creating a natural within-agent contrast (base_llm at sw=5 never switches, so any accuracy < 100% at sw=3 must be caused by the sw=3 knife-edge, not by prior commitment).

## 6. Failed Episode Analysis

Both failures occurred in full_cg_db/sw5 when the switch triggered late, leaving insufficient post-switch runway:

**Episode sw5/ep003:** The agent achieved 5 activations with the switch triggering at step 26. RC fired at step 29 (3 steps after switch). The agent submitted at step 31 with the pre-switch answer (conjunctive {A,B}). Only 5 post-switch steps — not enough time to explore the new rule space, even with the RC notification.

**Episode sw5/ep006:** The agent achieved 5 activations with the switch triggering at step 30. RC never fired. The agent submitted at step 33 with the pre-switch answer (conjunctive {A,B}). Only 3 post-switch steps — the switch happened so late that the DB had no opportunity to evaluate the changed pattern.

**Common mechanism:** Late activation of the 5th successful activation consumed most of the episode's step budget. The agent's final activations occurred in the high 20s, leaving only 3-5 steps before the agent decided to submit. In both cases, the agent had already formed high confidence in {A,B} from the pre-switch evidence and saw no reason to continue exploring.
