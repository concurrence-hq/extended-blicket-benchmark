## 1. Overview

Run 09 tests whether increasing the length of order-sensitive sequences from 2 objects (ceiling in Run 03: 100% all agents) to 3 or 4 objects produces architectural separation between `base_llm` and `full_cg_db`. The 2-object order condition decomposed into two trivial subproblems (identify 2 objects from 5, then determine binary order). Longer sequences change the task qualitatively: more objects to identify, exponentially more permutations (6 for 3 objects, 24 for 4), and order-identity confusability where incorrect ordering is indistinguishable from incorrect object selection.

Both sequences use non-alphabetical orderings (A->D->C and A->D->C->B) to avoid LLM alphabetical priors.

## 3. Root Cause: Detector Activation Failure

The dominant failure mode is not incorrect reasoning but **failure to ever activate the detector**. The order-sensitive rule requires objects to be placed in the exact required sequence. With 3 objects from 5, there are 60 possible ordered arrangements (5 x 4 x 3) and only one activates the detector. With 4 objects from 5, there are 120 possible arrangements and only one activates.

### 3.1 Activation Rates

| Cell | Agent | Condition | Episodes with ANY Activation | Episodes without Activation |
|------|-------|-----------|:----------------------------:|:---------------------------:|
| A | base_llm | 3-obj | **4/15 (27%)** | 11/15 (73%) |
| B | full_cg_db | 3-obj | **2/15 (13%)** | 13/15 (87%) |
| C | base_llm | 4-obj | **0/15 (0%)** | 15/15 (100%) |
| D | full_cg_db | 4-obj | **0/15 (0%)** | 15/15 (100%) |

**No 4-object episode across either agent ever activated the detector.** The agents never stumbled upon the sequence A->D->C->B in 15 episodes x ~85 steps = ~1,275 actions per cell. For 3-object ordering, activation occurred occasionally (6/30 = 20% of episodes), but only through fortunate placement sequences, not systematic order search.

### 3.2 What Happens Without Activation

Without detector activation, the agent receives only negative feedback ("The detector remains INACTIVE") for every combination it tries. This creates an information desert: the agent cannot distinguish the correct objects from incorrect ones, cannot identify that order matters, and has no signal to guide its search. The task degenerates into blind exploration of an exponentially large space with no positive feedback.

This explains the low answer submission rate (10/60 = 17% overall): agents run out of steps without gathering enough evidence to form a hypothesis. When they do submit, their answers reflect random guessing — Cell A's 4 submitted-but-wrong answers guessed 4-5 objects with incorrect orders, indicating the agent suspected order was relevant but had no evidence to determine the correct sequence.

### 3.3 The Information Bottleneck

The 2-object order condition (Run 03) succeeds because the agent reliably finds the pair {A, B} through standard conjunctive reasoning (both objects needed), then needs only 1 additional test to determine order (A then B, or B then A). The probability of accidentally placing A then B during exploration is high (~50% per conjunctive test).

The 3-object condition fails because:
1. The agent must first discover which 3 of 5 objects are relevant — but without ever seeing the detector activate, it cannot eliminate distractors
2. Even if it guesses the correct set, it needs to test 6 permutations to find the right order
3. The probability of accidentally placing A->D->C in sequence is ~1.7% per placement round

The 4-object condition is worse on every dimension: more objects to identify, 24 permutations to search, and ~0.8% chance of accidental activation per round.

## 7. DB and CG Analysis (Cells B and D)

### 7.1 CG State Trajectories

Without detector activation, the CG cannot transition into states that would be useful for order reasoning. The ORDER_TESTING state, which drives systematic permutation search, requires prior detection of order-inconsistent evidence (same objects, different activation results). Since the detector never activates in most episodes, no inconsistency is ever observed, and the CG stays in early exploration states.

### 7.2 DB Fire Patterns

The DB's `order_hypothesis` trigger fires when the trigger model detects anomalous activation patterns. With zero activations in 4-object episodes and near-zero in most 3-object episodes, the DB has no anomalies to detect. The DB mechanism is effectively inert — not because it malfunctions, but because the prerequisite signal (detector activation) never occurs.

### 7.3 Why full_cg_db Performs No Better (or Worse) Than base_llm

The CG/DB architecture is designed to help agents reason about evidence. When there is no evidence (zero activations), the architecture has nothing to work with. The overhead of structured exploration (CG-driven systematic combination testing, DB trigger evaluations) consumes steps without producing useful information, while the base_llm's less structured exploration has a marginally higher chance of stumbling upon the correct sequence through lucky placement order.

This explains the counterintuitive result that base_llm slightly outperforms full_cg_db on 3-object ordering (1/15 vs 0/15): the CG's systematic approach (test singles, then pairs, then triples) generates orderly but non-activating placements, while the base_llm's more chaotic exploration occasionally produces the correct sequence by accident.

## 9. Decision Criteria Assessment

### 9.1 Pre-Registered Questions (from Planning Prompt Section 4.1)

**Q1: Does the 3-object ordering break the ceiling?**
Yes — dramatically. Accuracy collapsed from 100% (Run 03) to 6.7% / 0.0% for base_llm / full_cg_db respectively. The ceiling is thoroughly broken.

**Q2: Does the 4-object ordering produce separation or floor?**
Floor. Both agents at 0/15 (0.0%). Zero detector activations across all 30 episodes. No evidence of any reasoning about order.

**Q3: Does the `order_hypothesis` DB fire appropriately?**
Moot. The DB has no signal to work with. Without detector activations, there are no activation anomalies for the trigger model to detect. The DB mechanism is effectively dormant.

**Q4: What are the failure modes?**
Overwhelmingly timeout/no-answer (83%). When answers are submitted, the failure is at object identification (not ordering) — agents cannot determine which objects matter without positive feedback from the detector.

### 9.2 Decision Tree Navigation

From the planning prompt's decision tree (Section 4.2):

```
3-obj ceiling broken?
├── YES, separation exists
│   └── ...
├── NO, both at ceiling
│   └── ...
└── MIXED → ...
```

The result does not cleanly match any branch: the ceiling is broken for **both** agents with no separation. This is closest to:

```
3-obj ceiling broken? → YES
└── Both below ceiling, no separation → increase N or consider intermediate difficulty
```

Both agents are well below 60% on 3-object ordering, which the planning prompt flagged as potentially "too hard at 75 steps." The core issue is not step budget but the information structure of the task.

### 9.3 Structural Analysis: Why This Is Fundamentally Hard

The order-sensitive task has a **cold start problem**. With conjunctive rules (Runs 01-08), agents can identify relevant objects incrementally: place A -> inactive, place B (with A) -> ACTIVE. Each test provides binary feedback that narrows the hypothesis space. The agent bootstraps from individual object tests to identify the relevant set.

With order-sensitive rules, the same initial tests (place A -> inactive, place B -> inactive, place A+B -> ACTIVE for the correct order) require the agent to accidentally place objects in the correct sequence. For 2 objects, this happens naturally (~50% of the time when testing the pair). For 3+ objects, the probability of accidental correct ordering drops below 2%, creating an information vacuum that prevents any learning.

The task needs a mechanism to provide partial feedback — some signal that "you have the right objects but wrong order" to bridge the gap between identifying the relevant set and searching the permutation space.

## 10. Conclusions

### 10.1 Summary of Findings

1. **3-object ordering produces a complete floor effect for both agents.** Accuracy collapsed from 100% (2-object, Run 03) to 6.7% / 0.0% (3-object, Run 09). This is a phase transition, not a gradual decline.

2. **4-object ordering is strictly worse.** Zero correct answers, zero detector activations, zero answer submissions for full_cg_db. The task is information-theoretically intractable at this step budget.

3. **The CG/DB architecture provides no advantage.** The structured exploration architecture is designed to reason about evidence. When there is no evidence (no detector activations), the architecture has nothing to operate on. If anything, the CG's systematic-but-orderly exploration may be slightly worse than the base_llm's chaotic search for accidentally hitting the correct sequence.

4. **The bottleneck is not reasoning but information.** Agents fail not because they reason poorly about evidence, but because they never receive positive evidence in the first place. The detector's all-or-nothing activation (correct sequence or nothing) creates an information desert.

5. **Answer submission rates are diagnostic.** 83% of episodes ended without any answer — agents know they lack evidence. When they do guess, their answers show no systematic understanding (wrong objects, random orders, over-inclusion).

### 10.2 CID Thesis Implications

- **Compositional architecture value depends on task information structure.** CG and DB provide clear advantages when the environment produces interpretable feedback (Runs 07-08: +22pp reasoning-eligible accuracy). When the environment produces zero useful feedback, composition is inert. This is a **boundary condition** for the CID thesis: architectural sophistication cannot compensate for information-theoretic task design.

- **Structured exploration can be a liability in sparse-feedback environments.** The CG's systematic approach (test singles, pairs, then triples) generates orderly placement sequences that have low probability of accidentally hitting the required order. Less structured exploration has higher variance and marginally higher activation probability. The same property that makes CG effective in evidence-rich tasks (systematic, thorough) makes it slightly worse in evidence-sparse tasks (predictable, low-variance).

### 10.3 Recommended Next Steps

The 3- and 4-object order-sensitive conditions are not viable as-is for discriminating architectures. Three possible paths forward:

**Option A: Add partial feedback mechanism.** Modify the environment to provide graded feedback (e.g., "N of your objects are in the correct position" or "the detector is partially active"). This would convert the information desert into a gradient that agents can follow. This changes the task from "discover the correct sequence by accident" to "systematically narrow down the sequence using partial signals" — which is where the CG/DB architecture should excel.

**Option B: Increase step budget dramatically.** The 3-object condition had 27% activation rate for base_llm (4/15 episodes). With 200-300 steps, agents might activate the detector in most episodes. But this would test patience/step efficiency rather than reasoning quality, and costs would be prohibitive (~$5-8/episode).

**Option C: Use 2-object order as baseline, focus on hidden moderator.** Accept that order-sensitive is viable only at 2-object complexity (where it's at ceiling). The productive research direction remains the hidden moderator condition (Runs 07-08), where CG and DB provide statistically significant, mechanistically interpretable advantages through separable pathways. Additional order conditions add complexity without discrimination.

**Recommendation: Option C.** The hidden moderator condition (Runs 07-08) has already produced the paper's key result: CG drives reasoning quality (+20.6pp, p=0.038), DB prevents structural trap failures (29.8% -> 6.5% exactly-N rate), and the two mechanisms are separable and complementary. The order-sensitive condition at 2-object complexity establishes a ceiling baseline. The 3-4 object order results are a useful boundary finding (reporting that the task collapses) but not a productive direction for further investment.
