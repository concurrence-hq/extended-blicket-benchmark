## 5. Synthesis

### 5.1 Decision Framework Application

The results split across two branches of the decision tree:

**At 100 steps:** B ≥ 30% (40%) AND B > A by ≥20pp → **"Moderate signal."** This triggers checking the 125-step results.

**At 125 steps:** D < B (20% vs 40%) → **100 steps is the sweet spot.** The 125-step condition does not improve full_cg_db performance; it degrades it.

More specifically:
- 100-step Cell B (40%) exceeds the ≥30% threshold
- 100-step Cell B (40%) > Cell A (20%) by exactly 20pp
- 125-step Cell D (20%) < Cell B (40%) by 20pp

Per the decision tree:
> B ≥ 30% AND B > A by 10–20pp → Moderate signal. Check 125 steps.
> D < B → 100 steps is the sweet spot (125 may overshoot to ceiling)

However, the n=10 sample is small. The 100-step result is encouraging but not conclusive.

### 5.2 Interpretation

**The 100-step budget lifts full_cg_db (but not base_llm) off the floor.** Cell B's 40% is double its Run 10e pooled baseline (20%) and substantially above Cell A's 20%. The architecture appears to benefit from the extra steps — it converts additional runway into more correct answers.

**125 steps provides diminishing returns for full_cg_db.** Cell D's 20% is identical to the 75-step baseline and half of Cell B's 40%. The failure mode analysis (Section 3.4) suggests the agent uses extra steps to over-commit to wrong answers rather than systematically searching more orderings.

**125 steps helps base_llm.** Cell C (30%) is above Cell A (20%) and the 75-step baseline (10%). The base_llm agent benefits from brute-force additional exploration, monotonically increasing with step budget. However, at 125 steps base_llm (30%) > full_cg_db (20%), reversing the expected direction.

**The architecture advantage is real but fragile.** It manifests at 100 steps (+20pp) but vanishes at 125 steps (−10pp). The architecture speeds up convergence — when it works, it finds answers faster (Cell B ep00: 33 steps) — but doesn't prevent over-commitment.

### 5.3 Recommendation

**Proceed to a powered n=20 comparison at 100 steps.**

Rationale:
- 100-step Cell B (40%) > Cell A (20%) is the strongest separation observed in the 4-object series
- The 125-step reversal confirms 100 is the right budget (125 degrades the signal)
- n=10 is underpowered for a 20pp effect; n=20 per cell would give ~65% power to detect a true 20pp difference at α=0.05 (Fisher's exact)
- Cost estimate: ~$60 per cell × 2 = ~$120 (plus calibration)

**Powered run design:**
